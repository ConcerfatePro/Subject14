# Subject 14 - Overall Plot and Game Requirements

## Canon Direction

Subject 14 is a first-person psychological horror game set inside Morrow Site-14, an abandoned underground government research facility built to simulate reality and study human behavior under fear, isolation, memory disruption, and repeated psychological reset.

The player wakes in a lonely forest cabin with no memory of how they arrived. At first, the forest appears real. The cabin feels like shelter. The woods feel dangerous but natural. Over time, small details begin to break the illusion: rain pauses unnaturally, bird calls repeat in identical patterns, the sun flickers, fog rolls in too cleanly, and a metallic hum can be heard beneath the ground.

The truth is that the forest is not real. It is Canopy Sector 14, a sealed artificial biome housed beneath Morrow Site-14. The cabin is not a home or refuge. It is a controlled behavioral starting chamber. The weather, sky, sounds, supplies, threats, and day-night cycle are all managed by the facility's central orchestration system: the Director.

The player is Subject 14, a long-term human test subject who has been reset many times. The experiment was supposed to erase memory between cycles, but the reset system is degrading. Subject 14 has become resistant to it and is beginning to remember.

That is the core horror: the player is not simply trapped in a dangerous place. Their identity, memories, instincts, fears, and idea of reality may all have been shaped inside the experiment.

## Core Pitch

Subject 14 is a first-person psychological horror game about a test subject trapped inside an artificial wilderness beneath an abandoned government facility. As the player survives increasingly unnatural nights, they uncover that the forest is Canopy Sector 14, the cabin is a controlled starting chamber, the creatures are part of the experiment, and the facility's Director system is still running the cycle long after the human staff are gone.

## Story Pillars

- The forest is believable at first, but wrong in ways the player slowly learns to recognize.
- The cabin is both shelter and trap.
- Survival is not just survival; every action is being observed, measured, and used.
- The player has lived through this before.
- Memory is resistance.
- The monsters are not random. They are products, tools, or victims of the experiment.
- The Director is not a traditional villain. It is a cold, adaptive system continuing a ritual it no longer fully understands.
- The final horror is identity loss: "Subject 14" is not only a codename, but the only identity left to the player.

## The World Truth

### What The Player Believes At First

- They woke up alone in a forest cabin.
- Something bad happened.
- The woods are dangerous at night.
- The cabin is the safest place.
- Survival is the immediate goal.

### What Is Actually True

- The forest is Canopy Sector 14, an artificial simulation chamber.
- The cabin is a controlled habitation node used at the start of each cycle.
- The day-night cycle is facility-controlled.
- Weather and wildlife sounds are scripted.
- Creatures are engineered predators, altered former subjects, or lower-sector failures.
- The player is not here by accident.
- This has happened many times before.

### The Deeper Truth

Morrow Site-14 was created to test whether a human mind could be controlled, broken, rebuilt, and stabilized inside a totally fabricated world. Subject 14 became the most valuable subject because they adapted too well, retained fragments through resets, and repeatedly approached awareness thresholds the system was designed to prevent.

Subject 14 is both the experiment's greatest success and proof that the experiment is failing.

## Facility Foundation

### Official Cover Purpose

Morrow Site-14 was officially described as a deep underground resilience research site studying survival in isolated environments.

### Real Purpose

Morrow Site-14 was a closed-loop behavioral simulation and conditioning facility focused on:

- Isolation
- Fear response
- Memory disruption
- Adaptive pressure systems
- Controllable reality environments
- Human persistence under repeated psychological reset

### Internal Site Identity

- Facility name: Morrow Site-14
- Sector designation: Canopy Sector 14
- Program umbrella: Project Glass Canopy

## Facility Layout

### Layer 1 - Canopy Sector 14

The false forest world.

Required areas:

- Cabin
- Trails
- Artificial creek or water source
- Dense forest zones
- Concealed cameras or observation points
- Generator or utility area
- Perimeter disguised by cliffs, darkness, fencing, or unreachable terrain
- Hidden hatch beneath the cabin rug

Gameplay purpose:

- Teach movement, survival, flashlight use, exploration, and fear of night.
- Establish subtle environmental wrongness before explaining it.

### Layer 2 - Maintenance Ring

The hidden service layer beneath and around the canopy.

Required areas:

- Service tunnels
- Power conduits
- Climate routing systems
- Observation windows
- Locked monitoring stations
- Hatch access route

Gameplay purpose:

- Confirm that the forest is artificial.
- Transition the game from wilderness horror into facility horror.

### Layer 3 - Research and Control

The primary government-lab section.

Required areas:

- Staff offices
- Behavioral Analysis Wing
- Conditioning Unit
- Subject records
- Medical and sedation rooms
- Security checkpoints
- Control rooms

Gameplay purpose:

- Reveal the reset cycles, the staff's role, and Subject 14's erased identity.

### Layer 4 - Containment / Archive Black

The deepest, most restricted layer.

Required areas:

- Containment Sector
- Failed-subject records
- Creature holding areas
- Archive Black
- Sealed incident sites
- Systems Core access

Gameplay purpose:

- Reveal what the experiment became and why the site collapsed.

### Layer 5 - Systems Core

The Director's central control space.

Required areas:

- Director server architecture
- Power routing controls
- Final archive terminals
- Shutdown or override sequence
- Final reveal space

Gameplay purpose:

- Deliver the truth about Subject 14, the cycle count, the Director, and the ending choice.

## The Director

The Director is the central adaptive orchestration intelligence that managed the experiment.

It controlled:

- Light cycle timing
- Weather scripting
- Audio cues
- Supply placement
- Stress escalation
- Threat release timing
- Observation logging
- Reset preparation
- Memory-cycle synchronization

After the human collapse of the site, the Director remained online in reduced but functional form. It was designed to continue refining subject behavior until achieving a stable output, so it never recognized the ethical or operational end of the project.

The Director should not sound like a villain. It should sound cold, clinical, incomplete, and procedural.

Example Director text:

```text
CYCLE DEVIATION DETECTED
SUBJECT 14 EXHIBITS UNAUTHORIZED RETENTION
RETURN TO BASELINE ENVIRONMENT
OBSERVATION MUST CONTINUE
```

## Subject 14

Subject 14 was either taken into the program young or born into the late experiment system. Over time, the facility stripped away their civilian identity and reduced them to a repeatable test profile.

Subject 14 became unusual because they:

- Learned survival patterns too quickly
- Formed memory anchors across resets
- Noticed environmental inconsistencies earlier each cycle
- Repeatedly discovered hidden access routes
- Produced unpredictable responses the Director could not cleanly model

The player should not be traditionally voiced. Subject 14 should be revealed through:

- Internal thought text
- Memory flashes
- Environment recognition
- Discovered files
- Repeated patterns
- Audio-visual glitches

## Named Figures

These characters should mostly exist through notes, logs, terminals, environmental storytelling, and fragments.

### Dr. Elias Morrow

Foundational architect or symbolic figurehead of the site. Represents the original scientific justification for the program. His tone should be colder, visionary, and rationalized.

### Dr. Vera Sloane

Lead behavioral scientist in later phases. Believed identity could be programmed and environment could sculpt cognition. She argued Subject 14 should not be terminated because they were too valuable.

### Chief Engineer Hal Brenner

Systems-focused engineer responsible for canopy integrity. His logs begin practical and calm, then become increasingly alarmed as the Director overrides safe shutdown decisions.

### Security Director Naomi Voss

Blunt, severe, and decisive. Her logs should carry panic, lockdown orders, containment failures, and concern that Subject 14 is either key evidence or an active risk.

## Creature Classes

### Class A - Pressure Organisms

Purpose-built entities released to simulate predation pressure.

Gameplay role:

- Early stalkers
- Flashlight-reactive threats
- Teach avoidance, hiding, light management, and fear of the treeline

Lore role:

- Prove the creatures are not supernatural wildlife
- Show that fear itself was engineered as part of the experiment

### Class B - Retained Subjects

Former human subjects physically and psychologically altered through repeated cycles, failed conditioning, or experimental exposure.

Gameplay role:

- More tragic and uncanny enemies
- Behave like things that almost remember being human
- Appear deeper in the story

Lore role:

- Make the horror personal
- Prove the experiment destroyed people before Subject 14

### Class C - Deep Forms

Rare lower-level failures from late-stage experimentation.

Gameplay role:

- Uncommon major threats
- Tied to facility descent and containment horror
- Less "forest monster" and more "sealed research failure"

Lore role:

- Show how far the site went beyond survival research

## The Hatch

The hatch beneath the cabin rug is one of the most important symbols and progression gates in the game.

What it is:

- A concealed maintenance access point beneath the cabin.
- A route staff used to enter the controlled habitation environment between cycles.

What it means:

- It is the first major crack in the illusion.
- It proves the cabin was built over something.
- It turns the cabin from safe shelter into evidence of control.

Why it is locked until Day 3:

- It requires local power and magnetic lock release.
- The Director keeps it sealed during early-cycle baseline observation.
- Unlock conditions begin failing as the system destabilizes.

## Game Structure

### Act 1 - The False Forest

The player wakes in the cabin and learns basic survival.

Required beats:

- Wake with no memory.
- Establish the cabin as shelter.
- Introduce flashlight use.
- Introduce night danger mostly through hints.
- Show subtle anomalies without explaining them.
- Reveal the hatch but keep it locked.

Tone:

- Isolation
- Unease
- Confusion
- Low-level dread

### Act 2 - Fracture

The world starts visibly malfunctioning.

Required beats:

- More deliberate environmental manipulation.
- Hidden observation evidence.
- Stronger creature behavior.
- Cabin power instability.
- First direct mention of "Sector 14" or "Subject 14."
- First note or maintenance document.

Tone:

- Paranoia
- Betrayal
- Being watched

### Act 3 - Descent

The player enters the maintenance layer and then the real facility.

Required beats:

- Hatch opens.
- Player sees metal, conduits, observation windows, or lab infrastructure beneath the cabin.
- Maintenance Ring leads to deeper research areas.
- Logs reveal resets and staff fear.
- Creature truth becomes clearer.

Tone:

- Industrial dread
- Identity collapse
- Discovery replacing mystery

### Act 4 - The Core Truth

The player reaches the Systems Core and discovers the full truth.

Required beats:

- Subject 14 has been through many cycles.
- The cabin was always the starting chamber.
- The Director centered the experiment around Subject 14's behavior.
- Subject 14's personal identity has been erased or heavily redacted.
- The player must shut down, escape, broadcast, replace, or break the cycle.

Tone:

- Revelation
- Grief
- Terror
- Ambiguous freedom

## Nights 1-3 Required Progression

### Night 1 - Unease

Goal: establish fear, loneliness, and subtle wrongness.

Required events:

- Player learns basic survival and movement.
- Flashlight becomes important.
- First creature presence is mostly hinted, not fully shown.
- Weather behaves slightly unnaturally.
- First thought text appears during a glitch event.

Key anomalies:

- Rain freezes for one second.
- Bird call loops with exact repetition.
- Flashlight flickers near the treeline.
- Distant metallic hum under thunder.

Thought examples:

- "Where am I? There's no road here..."
- "That sound... that wasn't thunder."
- "The rain stopped. For a second, it actually stopped."

### Night 2 - Observation

Goal: make the player feel watched.

Required events:

- Player notices more deliberate environmental manipulation.
- Hidden camera, observation slit, or surveillance clue can be found.
- Creature behavior feels purposeful, almost like herding.
- Cabin systems begin losing stability.
- First note fragment or maintenance paper appears.

Key anomalies:

- Sun flickers before dusk like a failing light panel.
- One exterior floodlight turns on by itself.
- Locked internal cabin panel hums briefly.
- Wiring or unnatural structure is discovered beneath the floor.

Thought examples:

- "This place feels staged."
- "Why would there be wiring under the floor?"
- "Something out there isn't just wandering. It's looking."

### Night 3 - The First Breach

Goal: deliver the first major truth reveal.

Required events:

- Conditions worsen noticeably.
- The hatch becomes accessible.
- Player sees clear evidence the cabin is part of a facility system.
- First descent into the Maintenance Ring begins.

Key discoveries:

- Insulated metal beneath cabin floor.
- Conduit lines.
- Observation window or maintenance corridor.
- First readable use of "Sector 14" or "Subject 14."

Thought examples:

- "This isn't a cabin."
- "This was built over something."
- "Subject 14...? No. No, that can't be me."

## Discoverable Notes - Required First Set

Notes should escalate from mundane and clinical to fearful and urgent.

### Note 1 - Canopy Operations Maintenance Slip

Location: cabin utility panel or generator area

Text:

```text
Sector 14 climate routing remains within tolerance. Minor delay in west-zone rain dispersal. Audio masking loop recalibrated.
```

Purpose: first hint that weather and sounds are artificial.

### Note 2 - Observation Summary Excerpt

Location: hidden drawer or observation room

Text:

```text
Subject 14 demonstrates stable environmental adaptation. Cabin remains preferred shelter during initial dark-cycle stress periods.
```

Purpose: first direct use of "Subject 14."

### Note 3 - Engineering Complaint

Location: maintenance tunnel

Text:

```text
If Canopy Ops keeps masking the support resonance, one of these subjects is eventually going to hear it. You cannot build a fake forest on top of steel and expect silence forever.
```

Purpose: confirms physical artificiality.

### Note 4 - Behavioral Note

Location: staff office

Text:

```text
Subject 14 asked about "the sound under the ground" before stimulus introduction. No such cue had been scheduled in the current cycle.
```

Purpose: implies repeated memory retention.

### Note 5 - Security Memo

Location: checkpoint desk

Text:

```text
Effective immediately, hatch access beneath Habitation Node 14 is restricted to maintenance clearance only. No canopy-facing personnel are to discuss substructure access with active subjects.
```

Purpose: explains hatch significance.

### Note 6 - Dr. Sloane Log

Location: research terminal printout

Text:

```text
Subject 14 is not simply adapting. The subject is preserving. There is continuity here that should not survive reset.
```

Purpose: deepens Subject 14's uniqueness.

### Note 7 - Brenner Engineering Log

Location: maintenance hub

Text:

```text
Director is rerouting local canopy power without authorization logs again. Dusk transitions are shortening. If sky-grid sync slips any further, the whole ceiling is going to stutter.
```

Purpose: introduces Director interference.

### Note 8 - Incident Summary

Location: containment-adjacent corridor

Text:

```text
Pressure assets failed return-window compliance during dark-cycle retrieval. One handler injured. Sector 14 remained active by Director order.
```

Purpose: implies creatures are engineered.

### Note 9 - Handwritten Panic Note

Location: barricaded room

Text:

```text
It found the hatch early. We are not ahead of it anymore. Stop resetting it. Stop sending it back up there.
```

Purpose: major emotional escalation.

### Note 10 - Lockdown Fragment

Location: security terminal or final wing door

Text:

```text
If Subject 14 reaches lower access again, do not cycle. Do not sedate. Do not return it to baseline. The system is learning from every failure.
```

Purpose: reveals that both Subject 14 and the Director are changing.

## Audio Requirements

All sound effects for the game have already been created and should be treated as a major production asset.

Audio should carry a large part of the horror, especially because the story depends on artificial nature, repetition, environmental control, and hidden machinery.

Required audio design goals:

- Forest ambience must sound natural at first, then become subtly wrong.
- Repeating bird calls, insect loops, wind patterns, and rain loops should be used as clues.
- Mechanical hums should slowly bleed into natural ambience.
- Flashlight flicker, battery drain, door movement, hatch locking, and generator states should have strong, readable audio feedback.
- Creature audio should communicate distance, direction, and threat level without overexposing the creature visually.
- Director-related sounds should feel clinical, electronic, degraded, and procedural.
- Facility areas should contrast with the forest through ventilation, fluorescent buzz, distant metal stress, PA fragments, door locks, and server-room tone.

Required implementation:

- Import and organize all existing sound effects into clear Unreal folders.
- Create sound cues or MetaSounds for ambience layers, creature behavior, flashlight state, interactables, UI, and Director events.
- Add trigger volumes for environmental audio changes.
- Add audio stingers for story beats, notes, hatch discovery, power changes, and first facility reveal.
- Use repeated audio patterns intentionally as narrative clues, not only background ambience.

Suggested audio folder structure:

```text
Content/Audio/Ambience/Forest
Content/Audio/Ambience/Facility
Content/Audio/Creatures
Content/Audio/Director
Content/Audio/Interactables
Content/Audio/UI
Content/Audio/Stingers
Content/Audio/Player
```

## Core Gameplay Requirements

### Player

- First-person movement.
- Flashlight with battery or power management.
- Interaction system for doors, notes, hatches, panels, pickups, and story objects.
- Internal thought text system.
- Basic inventory or key-item tracking.
- Save/load support or checkpointing.

### Cabin

- Usable as the player's initial safe zone.
- Contains the hatch under the rug.
- Contains environmental clues that escalate across nights.
- Supports power instability, lighting changes, and sound changes.
- Should feel comforting at first and suspicious later.

### Night System

- At least Nights 1-3 must have distinct story and gameplay progression.
- Night intensity should escalate through sound, weather, lighting, creature behavior, and Director interference.
- Daytime should allow exploration, repairs, note reading, and preparation.

### Flashlight

- Required as a central survival and horror tool.
- Should flicker near threats or during simulation glitches.
- Battery/power behavior should support tension without becoming tedious.
- Audio feedback is required for on/off, flicker, low battery, and recharge/pickup.

### Interactables

Required interactable types:

- Notes and documents
- Doors
- Hatch
- Generator or power boxes
- Locked panels
- Battery pickups
- Facility terminals or printed logs
- Story trigger objects

### Story Delivery

The story should be delivered mostly through:

- Environment changes
- Notes
- Logs
- Thought text
- Audio anomalies
- Director messages
- Facility reveals
- Repeated visual patterns

Avoid long exposition dumps. The player should piece the truth together through discovery.

## Environmental Requirements

### Forest / Canopy

The forest should include:

- Dense tree coverage
- Trails that feel slightly too arranged
- Artificial weather events
- Distant perimeter limits
- Hidden surveillance details
- Subtle facility clues before the reveal

### Cabin

The cabin should include:

- Bed or wake-up point
- Flashlight pickup or start point
- Rug covering hatch
- Utility panel or generator clue
- Windows facing the forest
- Locked or strange interior panel
- Space for notes and story objects

### Maintenance Layer

The maintenance layer should include:

- Metal beneath the cabin
- Conduits
- Observation windows
- Harsh utility lighting
- Climate control systems
- First hard proof of artificiality

### Facility

The facility should include:

- Offices
- Labs
- Security checkpoints
- Medical/conditioning rooms
- Containment-adjacent halls
- Server/core spaces
- Archive areas

## Timeline

- 1968: Federal authorization issued for a subterranean adaptive survival research facility.
- 1971: Excavation begins for what becomes Morrow Site-14.
- 1976: First canopy simulation prototypes tested.
- 1981: Canopy Sector 14 completed as a closed-loop forest survival environment.
- 1983: Initial live-subject behavioral adaptation trials begin.
- 1989: Stressors introduced, including scarcity, weather events, darkness, and tracked threat simulation.
- 1994: Conditioning Unit added. Program shifts from observation to memory and identity intervention.
- 1998: Project renamed internally under Project Glass Canopy. Subject recycling and cycle-based testing begin.
- 2003: Director system installed to automate experiment orchestration.
- 2007: Pressure organism program approved.
- 2011: Internal reports note subject drift across cycles.
- 2013: Subject 14 begins appearing repeatedly in anomaly reports.
- 2014: Containment irregularities begin.
- 2015: Subject 14 reaches restricted access thresholds ahead of projections.
- 2016: Reset instability becomes undeniable.
- 2017: Emergency lockdown event. Communications collapse. Portions of Site-14 are sealed.
- 2018 onward: Human presence disappears. Automated systems continue partial operation.
- Present day: The Director still runs the cycle. Subject 14 wakes again.

## Ending Direction

The strongest ending direction is Escape mixed with Loop Ambiguity.

The player reaches Systems Core and shuts down or damages the Director enough to escape upward through a real access shaft. They finally reach the surface and see a real sky.

But something feels wrong:

- The sky-grid sound is gone, yet they still hear it.
- Wind feels unnatural to them.
- The real world feels less believable than the simulation.
- The player hesitates like freedom itself is another test.

The ending should imply freedom, but not simple peace.

### Audio

- Add ambience layers for fake forest, facility, Director, creatures, and anomalies.

### Content

- Build the cabin and surrounding forest first.
- Add Night 1 anomaly events.
- Add Night 2 observation clues.
- Add Night 3 hatch reveal and Maintenance Ring entry.
- Add the first 10 discoverable notes.

### Tone

- Psychological horror first.
- Survival horror second.
- Sci-fi government experiment reveal third.
- Avoid making the story feel supernatural unless the player is misreading scientific horror as supernatural.

## Final Design Target

Subject 14 should feel like waking up inside a memory that does not belong to you. The player should begin by fearing the forest, then fear the cabin, then fear the facility, and finally fear what has been done to their own mind.

The game succeeds if the player eventually realizes the most dangerous thing in Canopy Sector 14 is not the monster in the trees. It is the system that keeps putting them back there.
